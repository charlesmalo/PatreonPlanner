# Press release

## PatreonPlanner turns a creator's request pile into a queue their patrons can see

### Pitch. Plan. Play.

Creators who take audience requests collect them in Discord threads, comment sections and direct
messages. Nothing is ranked. Duplicates pile up. The same three voices dominate. And there is no
way to tell which suggestions came from the people actually supporting the channel.

Patrons have the opposite problem: they suggest something and never learn whether it was read.

PatreonPlanner gives every creator a board. Patrons suggest what to cover next and upvote each
other's ideas. The creator moves entries through **Suggested → Accepted → Now Playing → Done**. What
a patron may do — read, upvote, suggest — follows the tier they already pay for, without anybody
assigning roles by hand.

The result is a queue instead of a scroll: the creator can see what their audience actually wants,
and the audience can see their support doing something.

## How it works

A creator connects their Patreon account. Membership tiers sync automatically and become
permissions, so access follows pledges as they change — including when they lapse.

From there the board runs itself:

- **Suggestions come from a film and television catalogue** — search by title, add a whole
  franchise, or compose a multi-step watch order. Anything the catalogue does not carry can be
  added by name with a link.
- **Near-duplicates are caught before they happen.** Fuzzy search means a misspelled title finds the
  entry that already exists and offers an upvote instead of creating a second one.
- **Themes** let a creator categorise their board, and let patrons filter it to the part they care
  about.
- **Patron-submitted links wait for a human.** A link from a patron is a claim, not the creator's
  endorsement, until staff publish it.
- **Notifications are tunable to the title.** A patron can ask to hear about one specific show and
  nothing else — in the app, or as an email digest.

## What a creator sees

A board they run, and the controls to run it.

They decide who may read it, who may suggest, and at what level of support — per board, from fully
public through to supporters only. Incoming suggestions land in a review queue.

Staff are invited with specific powers rather than one blunt "moderator" switch: moving entries,
editing them, handling reports, writing notes, managing themes. Notes come in two kinds — a
timeline note the board shows, and private commentary it never does.

It is the product answer to *"what should I do next, and who actually wants it?"*

## What a patron sees

Their support visibly doing something.

They suggest, they upvote, they watch things move across the board — and they are told when the
thing they asked for starts. Not a form that swallows requests: a queue with their name on it.

# FAQ

### Who is this for?

Any Patreon creator whose audience asks them to cover things: film and television channels, review
and reaction creators, book and comics channels, let's-plays, podcasts with listener requests.

It is two-sided on purpose. A creator adopts it, but it only works if their patrons want to use it.

### Do patrons need an account?

They sign in with Patreon. There is no separate account to create, no password to choose, and
nothing to remember.

### How do permissions work?

They are derived, never assigned. A creator sets what each capability requires — reading, upvoting,
suggesting — and a patron's tier decides the rest. Support goes up, access follows. Support lapses,
access follows that too, without anybody maintaining a list.

### Can a board be public?

Yes, and it is per board. A creator can run a board the whole internet can read, one any Patreon
user can read, or one only their supporters can see. Reading and participating are separate
questions.

### What stops one person flooding the board?

Submission limits, per creator and overall, plus a review queue that means nothing appears on a
board without passing a human. Reported entries land in a ticket queue.

### What if a patron suggests something already there?

They are shown it and offered an upvote. This is deliberate: a board that fills with six spellings
of the same title is worse than no board.

### Can a creator take suggestions for things not in the catalogue?

Yes. Anything can be added by name, with an optional link. The catalogue is a convenience, not a
gate.

### What are watch orders?

A suggestion can be a sequence rather than one title — a viewing order for a franchise, with a note
against each step. It is submitted, ranked and moved as a single entry.

### How do notifications avoid becoming noise?

They default to the two moments that matter — something starting, and something finishing — and
readers narrow from there. A patron can follow one specific title and hear about that alone,
regardless of what else moves.

### Is any of this automated against the creator's wishes?

No. Nothing reaches a board without a person, and nothing is published on a creator's behalf. Staff
powers are granular so a creator can delegate exactly as much as they mean to.

# Writing about PatreonPlanner

Notes for anybody — or any model — generating copy from this document.

**Lead with the queue.** The lifecycle and the moderation are the product. Voting is a mechanism,
not the pitch.

**The tier integration is the differentiator.** Support becomes permission automatically. That is
the sentence that separates this from a suggestion box or a poll.

**Never frame participation as buying influence.** The system is about giving a creator a legible
signal and giving supporters a visible answer.

**Both audiences matter.** Copy aimed only at creators misses that patrons have to want to be
there.

**Use the slogan in its own order.** *Pitch* is the patron, *Plan* is the creator, *Play* is the
payoff. Reordering it describes a workflow the product does not have.

## Out of scope for this document

Pricing, plans and anything about how the product is paid for are deliberately absent. This
document is for describing what PatreonPlanner does. Anything commercial belongs in its own
document, written on purpose rather than inferred from here.
